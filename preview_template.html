Ring Finger Candles

Printable fingers that push into a candlestick cup in place of a candle and hold your rings. Rings below are placed where the model says each size comes to rest when slid down from the fingertip.

Printing and fitting

  • Plug: 22.0 mm across, 18 mm long, with three crush ribs to 22.6 mm. It fits standard 7/8 in (22 mm) taper-candle cups (the example candlestick you sent measures 22.3 mm at the bottom of the cup and 23.5 mm at the rim). Measure your own cup across the inside: if it is not 22 to 23 mm, the plug can be rebuilt to fit, or wrapped in a turn of tape.
  • Orientation: print standing up, plug down. The wax fingers print without supports, apart from light ones under the bent tip of the crooked finger with drips. The curled and hooked bone fingers need tree supports under the curl.
  • Layer height: 0.12 mm or finer shows the nail, the cuticle and the knuckle wrinkles. A resin printer shows them best and is the easiest base for a gloss or porcelain finish.
  • Never light the wick. PLA and resin burn and drip. It is for looks only.

Finishing

  • Glossy wax: print in translucent natural PLA or clear-ish resin, then brush on two thin coats of UV resin and cure them in sunlight. It levels over the layer lines and dries glass-clear. Thick coats run into the creases.
  • Drips and wick: the drip versions have the wax modelled in, and gloss makes it look wet. For real wax, drip it from a lit taper held 10 to 15 cm above the fingertip, and try a test print first, because PLA softens at about 55 to 60 °C. Glue 2 cm of braided cotton wick into the 2.2 mm hole and darken its end.
  • Porcelain: on a smooth base (resin, or filler primer sanded wet to 1500 grit), use gloss white enamel or white under a 2-part clear. Pink pastel chalk on the knuckles and fingertip before the clear coat gives a porcelain-doll look; crackle varnish gives antique craquelure; gold on the nails or along the creases suits gold rings.
  • Black glaze: gloss black over the same smooth base, like Victorian mourning jewellery. Silver rings stand out on it.
  • Bone: ivory paint or filament, a tea or umber wash wiped off the high spots, satin varnish.
  • Before the rings go on: let coatings cure fully, about a day for UV resin and a week for 2-part clear.

Poseable bone fingers

  • Four printed parts per finger, joined by short pins of 1.75 mm filament. The pins and the hinge sit inside the bones, so from outside you only see bone meeting bone.
  • Each pin goes in from one side and stops inside the bone. Fill that one small hole with filler or bone-coloured putty, sand and paint over it once you are happy with how the finger moves.
  • Snug holes hold the pose. Use the sliders under the 3D view to try it.
