# Ring Finger Candles

3D-printable fingers that push into a candlestick cup in place of a candle and hold rings.
There are two styles:

- **Female fingers:** slender, long and evenly tapered, with small soft knuckles, faint skin detail and a long oval nail that runs into a rounded free edge. Each comes three ways: with skin detail, with melted wax, and smooth and idealised for a porcelain finish.
- **Male wax fingers:** broader, with stronger knuckles and creases. Each comes plain or with melted wax.
- **Bone fingers:** a skeletal finger made of a metacarpal head and the proximal, middle and distal phalanx, on a puddle of wax. It comes as a poseable four-part kit pinned with filament, or as one-piece prints in three fixed poses.

Every model in `out/` is generated by `fingers.py` from scratch. None of the uploaded models are copied into them, so the files are free to use.

## The wax finger

The finger is one continuous tube along a centre line that bends gradually through each joint, so the joints have no steps.

- **Cross-section:** a little boxy on the back and round on the palm side.
- **Palm side:** a soft pad on every segment, with thin creases at the joints (two at the middle joint, one at the end joint).
- **Back:** a bony bump at each knuckle with a patch of wrinkles that fades out in an oval.
- **Tip:** a fleshy pad and a nail set into skin folds, with a curved cuticle, side grooves and a slight free edge.
- **Skin:** a faint unevenness all over, so a gloss coat does not look machined.

Each drip is traced downhill over the skin from the edge of the melted layer.
It is a flat ribbon of wax, about three times wider than it is high, that thickens into a small teardrop.
The drips vary in length, so they stop at different heights.
They widen the top of the finger, so on the drip versions rings stop higher.

## Fitting your candleholder

The plug is **22.0 mm across and 18 mm long**, with three small crush ribs that make it 22.6 mm.
That fits the standard 7/8 in (22 mm) taper-candle cup.
The example candlestick measured 22.3 mm at the bottom of its cup and 23.5 mm at the rim.

Measure the inside of your own cup across its widest part, and its depth.

- **22 to 23 mm across:** print as is.
- **Slightly bigger:** wrap the plug once with tape, as you would a loose candle.
- **Anything else:** rebuild the plug to your measurement:
  `python fingers.py --cup 20.5` (diameter in mm), or `--plug-len 12` for a shallow cup.
  This takes about a minute per finger.

The bone fingers and the drip versions have a wax collar 26.5 mm across that sits on the cup's rim. Turn it off with `--collar off`.

## Where rings come to rest

The script slides a rigid ring with a 3 mm band down each finger from the tip.
At every position it measures the smallest circle around the finger and records where each US size first touches.
The ring turns to stay square to the finger's centre line, but it cannot tip any further.
A real ring can tip, so it may get a little further over a bent joint.
Sizes that pass everything sit on the cup rim.
US sizes use the standard inner diameter, 11.63 mm + 0.8128 mm per size (US 7 = 17.3 mm).

## Printing

- **Orientation:** upright, plug down, on the flat end of the plug. No brim is needed.
- **Layers:** 0.12 mm or finer brings out the nail, the cuticle and the knuckle wrinkles. A resin printer shows them best and is the easiest base for a gloss or porcelain finish.
- **One-piece bone fingers:** each joint has a 0.7 mm gap around a hidden 4 to 6 mm core, so the finger prints as one piece and does not move.
  On a filament printer the gap may fuse in places; that still reads as a joint.

## Finishing

**Glossy wax.** This look reads as a real candle.

1. Print in translucent "natural" PLA or a clear-ish resin, so light goes a little way in like it does in wax.
2. Brush on a thin coat of UV resin and cure it in sunlight or under a UV lamp.
   It levels over layer lines and dries glass-clear and glossy.
   Two thin coats are better than one thick one, because thick coats run and collect in the creases.
3. Alternatively, use a self-levelling 2-part epoxy coating made for 3D prints, or a gloss clear spray over a smooth print.

**Drips and wick.** The drip versions have the wax modelled in, and a gloss coat makes it look wet.
For real wax, drip it from a lit taper held 10 to 15 cm above the fingertip, so each drop cools a little before it lands.
Try it on a test print first: PLA softens at about 55 to 60 °C. PETG and most resins take the heat better.
Glue a 2 cm piece of braided cotton wick into the 2.2 mm hole. Stiffen and curl the end with a drop of glue or wax.
A dab of dark brown or black at the very end makes it look burnt.

**Porcelain.** Use the `-smooth` female fingers. Porcelain needs the smoothest base, so resin prints are best.

- On filament prints, use filler primer, then sand wet from 400 up to 1500 grit until the layer lines are gone.
- Then apply gloss white enamel, or white base plus a 2-part gloss clear.
- **Porcelain doll:** dust a little pink pastel chalk on the knuckles and the fingertip pad before the clear coat.
- **Antique:** a 2-part crackle varnish over the white gives fine craquelure; rub a little umber into the cracks.
- **With gold:** gold leaf or a gold paint pen on the nails, or along the palm creases like kintsugi, goes well with gold rings.
- **Black glaze:** gloss black over the same smooth base looks like Victorian mourning jewellery and makes silver rings stand out.

**Real porcelain.** A printed finger can serve as the master for a plaster mould and be slip-cast in porcelain.
Porcelain shrinks about 12 to 15 % in firing, so scale the master up by about 1.15.

**Bone.** Use ivory or bone-white filament or paint. Then apply a tea or thinned raw-umber wash and wipe it off the high spots, so the joints and grooves stay darker.
Finish with satin varnish, not gloss.

**Rings.** Let every coating cure fully before rings go on: UV resin about a day, 2-part clear about a week.
Otherwise rings can stick or mark.
Keep sulfur-containing modelling clay away from silver, because it tarnishes it.

**Never light the wick.** PLA and resin burn and drip; the wick is only there for looks.

## Curling female fingers

`out/curl/` holds the slender finger and the little finger in three rigid pieces each (base with the plug, middle, fingertip), detailed or `-smooth`.
They are joined at the two knuckles with hidden pins of 1.75 mm filament, so the finger curls and holds its pose.

- **Back of the finger:** the lower piece ends in a rounded knuckle that the piece above slides over, so the back stays closed at any angle and the knuckle shows as the finger curls. You see a hairline at each knuckle crease.
- **Palm side:** a rigid finger cannot squash like skin, so the palm crease is a groove about 2.4 mm wide that closes as the finger curls. Inside it the hinge rings are visible when the finger is straight.
- **Range:** each knuckle curls up to about 72 to 75 degrees.
- **Pins:** one pin per knuckle goes in from the side and stops inside (lengths are in `out/curl/*.json`). Fill the small entry hole and paint over it.
- **Printing:** each piece upright, no supports. Coat each piece separately and keep the coat thin on the joint faces so they still slide.

## Poseable bone finger

`out/poseable/` is a bone finger in four parts joined by pins cut from ordinary 1.75 mm filament.
It bends at the knuckle, the middle joint and the end joint, and holds whatever pose you put it in.

| File | Part | Print |
|---|---|---|
| `bone-base.stl` | plug, wax collar and the knuckle head of the hand bone | upright, plug down |
| `bone-proximal.stl` | first finger bone | lying on its side, tree supports |
| `bone-middle.stl` | second finger bone | lying on its side, tree supports |
| `bone-distal.stl` | fingertip bone | lying on its side, tree supports |

The bones are laid on their side so the pin holes print vertically and come out round.

**How a joint works.** Nothing mechanical shows on the finished finger.
The bone below ends in two rounded knobs with a thin valley between them, like a real finger bone.
Inside, the valley widens into a slot for a tab on the base of the bone above.
A rounded bump on the back of each bone's base, where the tendon attaches on a real bone, hangs over the joint and hides it from behind.
The pin goes in from one side only, through the first knob and the tab, and stops inside the second knob.

**Pins.** Cut three pieces of 1.75 mm filament: 14 mm for the knuckle, 13 mm for the middle joint, 10 mm for the end joint.

1. Push each pin into its entry hole until it stops.
2. If a pin will not go in, run a 1.8 mm drill through the hole. Stop at the blind end.
   The hole in the base part prints horizontally, so it usually needs this.
3. Fill the entry hole with a dab of filler or bone-coloured epoxy putty, sand it smooth and paint over it.
   The pins can no longer come out after that, so pose-test the finger first.

**Holding the pose.** The holes are 1.8 mm, so the pins are snug. That friction holds the pose, including the weight of a few rings.
If a joint is too loose, swap in a pin of slightly thicker filament or put a drop of nail varnish on the pin.
If it is too stiff, drill the tab hole out to 1.85 mm.
`python poseable.py --pin-hole 1.85` makes all the joints looser.

**How far each joint bends.** These ranges come from turning each joint in 2.5° steps and checking that the parts never overlap:

| Joint | Bends towards the palm | Bends backwards |
|---|---|---|
| knuckle | up to 65° | up to -0° |
| middle joint | up to 80° | up to 5° |
| end joint | up to 85° | up to 8° |

The bump on the back stops each joint from bending backwards, much as on a real finger.

**Where rings rest** in three poses, in mm above the cup rim:

| Pose (knuckle / middle / end) | US 4 | US 5 | US 6 | US 7 | US 8 | US 9 | US 10 | US 11 | US 12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| straight (4° / 10° / 14°) | 69 | 21 | 20 | 20 | 19 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3 |
| curled (8° / 40° / 55°) | 66 | 20 | 20 | 20 | 19 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3 |
| hook (0° / 25° / 75°) | 68 | 21 | 20 | 20 | 19 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3 |

`pose-*.stl` are the assembled finger in those poses, for viewing only, not for printing.
In `preview.html`, the middle finger of the "Poseable bones" scene has sliders for the three joints.

**Wax fingers** can bend only if the skin is cut at the joints, which leaves visible seams like a porcelain ball-jointed doll.
That suits a porcelain finish but not the wax-candle look, so the wax fingers stay one piece.

## Making your own finger

`adapt.py` turns any finger STL into a candle finger. It fills the model solid, adds the plug and drills a wick hole.
The model must stand upright with its base at the bottom.

    python adapt.py some-finger.stl out/my-finger.stl --cut 2 --cup 22

A model with a closed surface keeps that surface exactly.
For example, a 3D scan of a real finger made with a phone app would come out looking like the cast wax fingers in your photo.
Anything else is rebuilt from voxels at 0.2 mm.

The Flexy-Finger prosthesis (Gyrobot, CC BY-NC-SA) was tested this way and converts cleanly.
It is not included because it is a functional prosthesis, with a hinge recess on the palm side and tendon slots.
It looks mechanical next to the generated fingers.

## Changing the fingers

`fingers.py` builds each finger as a signed distance field, meshes it with marching cubes at 0.2 mm and checks that the result is watertight.
The presets are at the bottom of the file:

- joint bends (`mcp`, `pip`, `dip` in degrees towards the palm, and `tilt`, `pip_side`, `dip_side` sideways);
- width and length;
- the number of drips and their random seed (`drips`, `drip_seed`);
- bone scale and joint gap.

    pip install numpy scipy scikit-image trimesh fast_simplification shapely networkx manifold3d
    python fingers.py                      # all presets into out/
    python fingers.py --only bone-curled   # one preset
    python poseable.py                     # the poseable bone kit into out/poseable/
    python preview.py                      # rebuild preview.html

The bone proportions are approximate adult index-finger phalanges, scaled up 1.3 times so the joints are wide enough to hold rings.
